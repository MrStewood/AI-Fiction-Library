# Original Prompt

> Preserve the user's prompt verbatim below. Do not edit after lock except via explicit change control.

```text
Write an original literary short story (target 4,000–5,000 words; about 4–6 scenes).

Premise:
An adult child returns to their childhood home for one night to help an aging parent empty the house before a closing the next morning. They must decide what to keep and what to discard. Sorting becomes a confrontation over one unspoken family event that both have avoided for years.

Constraints for this pilot:
- One central conflict only: whether (and how) they finally name that event before the house is sold.
- Cast: exactly two speaking characters (adult child + parent). No new major characters.
- Locations: at most two (the house interior + optional short threshold/porch/driveway). Prefer mostly one house.
- Timeline: roughly dusk to dawn, or one evening into morning.
- No subplot, romance arc, crime plot, supernatural twist, or institutional conspiracy.
- Tone: literary / contemporary realist; PG-13; non-graphic.
- End on a hard character choice with lasting consequence (even if emotionally unresolved). Do not tidy it into a neat moral lesson.

Story must show:
- concrete sensory grounding (objects, rooms, weather, bodies);
- dialogue with subtext (deflection, business, withheld speech);
- clear goal / opposition / agency for the protagonist in each scene;
- causal scene-to-scene pressure so a reader wants the next scene.

Deliver a complete short story, not a novel outline.
```
