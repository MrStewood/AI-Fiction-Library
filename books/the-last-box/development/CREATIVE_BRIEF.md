# Creative Brief

## One-sentence concept

On the last night before their childhood home is sold, an adult child's attempt to sort boxes with their aging parent forces both to finally speak the family event they have protected for years—changing what each is willing to keep, discard, or hand back.

## Format and target length

- Format: short story
- Target word count: 4,500
- Word range: 4,000–5,000
- Chapters required: no
- Typical scene count: 4–6 scenes
- Typical scene length: roughly 700–1,000 words per scene
- Delivery expectation: one complete story, not outline or synopsis

## Audience

- Primary: adult literary-fiction readers who respond to emotionally grounded, realistic family stories
- Secondary: readers of character-driven short fiction interested in regret, memory, obligation, and the costs of silence
- Reading context: designed for single-sitting or evening reading, where compression and emotional clarity matter more than plot complexity

## Genre and story promise

- Genre: contemporary literary realism
- Promise: a compressed, emotionally honest confrontation between two people who have spent years avoiding a shared wound
- Subgenre alignment: domestic realism / family drama
- Contract with the reader: the story will not use gimmicks, melodrama, or contrived revelations; it will earn its pressure through setting, object, dialogue, and choice

## Tone and emotional experience

- Tone: restrained, precise, quietly bruised
- Emotional arc: from guarded caretaking and practical sorting to exposure, rupture, and a difficult, ambiguous resolution
- Atmosphere: dust, late heat or early autumn chill, boxes, taped furniture, stale rooms, threshold light, the feeling of a house emptied one step too late
- Emotional experience for the reader: intimacy, tension, melancholy, recognition, and the ache of an ending that feels true rather than tidy

## POV and tense

- POV: adult child
- Tense: past tense
- Focal distance: close and intimate, with enough exterior observation to anchor rooms, weather, bodies, and objects
- Why this choice: the story depends on the gap between what the child notices, what they avoid saying, and what the parent will not volunteer

## Required elements

- Two speaking characters only: adult child and aging parent
- One central conflict only: whether and how they finally name the avoided event before the house closes
- Unspoken event class: a confession or disclosure one parent made that altered the child's understanding of the family, yet was never fully addressed
- The box must function as catalyst/pressure source, not as puzzle inventory or MacGuffin
- At most two locations: house interior and one brief threshold/porch/driveway moment
- One night timeline: roughly dusk to dawn or evening into morning
- Concrete sensory grounding in rooms, weather, objects, and the body
- Dialogue driven by subtext, deflection, business, and withheld speech
- Clear goal, opposition, and agency for the protagonist in each scene
- Scene-to-scene causal pressure that keeps the reader leaning forward
- An ending built on a hard character choice with lasting consequence, even if emotionally unresolved

## Forbidden elements

- No subplot, romance arc, crime plot, supernatural twist, or institutional conspiracy
- No large cast expansion, hidden relatives, or surprise late arrivals
- Nosentimental moralizing or therapeutic summary
- No puzzle-box structure, riddle clues, or treasure-hunt framing of the boxes
- No melodramatic confrontations that betray the realistic tone
- No tidy reconciliation that erases the cost of the avoided event
- No explicit graphic content beyond PG-13 emotional intensity
- No borrowing of a living author's distinctive style

## Content limits

- Rating: PG-13 / non-graphic default
- Allowed: emotional intensity, disappointment, regret, raised voices, withdrawal, grief, resentment, fatigue, ambivalence
- Avoided: detailed graphic violence, explicit sexuality, cruelty played for spectacle, broad comedy that breaks tone, and any context requiring content warnings beyond domestic emotional strain

## General prose attributes

- Concrete nouns and grounded sensory detail over abstraction
- Sentences shaped by pressure, withholding, and silence
- Dialogue that does the narrative work through what is avoided as much as what is said
- Scene logic driven by object, room, task, and emotional consequence
- Compression suitable to short fiction: no digressions, no decorative thematic speeches, no false breathing room

## Comparable qualities (no living-author imitation)

- Emotional restraint and moral clarity found in strong contemporary domestic realism
- Compression and layered silence reminiscent of classic American short fiction about family reckonings
- The emotional pressure of a family facing an irreversible decision in a single confined space
- Possession-like domestic unease, but without supernatural apparatus
- Raymond Carver-like pressure in everyday speech, withoutimitation of a specific author's mannerisms

## Explicit assumptions

- The unspoken family event is domestic and relational, not criminal or sensational
- The box matters because it forces proximity and triggers memory, not because it hides a physical mystery
- The parent is not reduced to frailty or caricature; the parent remains a fully realized agent with their own reasons
- The adult child wants resolution, but is also complicit in years of avoidance
- The house functions as a container for pressure, not as a symbol repeated without new consequence
- The story will remain strict about its two-character, one-night, one-conflict design

## Research needs

- Minimal research required; the story is character-driven and contemporary realist
- Useful texture only: the ordinary logistics of a house closing, end-of-day packing, and the physical fatigue of sorting through a home
- Sensory and psychological truth matter more than procedural detail
- No specialized professional, historical, scientific, or institutional research is required

## Definition of done

This brief is complete when it:

1. States one clear one-sentence concept
2. Locks the format, target length, POV, and tense
3. Defines audience, tone, genre, and story promise
4. Names the unspoken event class in one sentence
5. Frames the box as catalyst/pressure source rather than MacGuffin
6. Lists required and forbidden elements clearly
7. Gives the story architect, drafter, and reviewers enough to build premise, bible, beats, and scenes without added invention
