# Premise Package

## Logline

On the last night before their childhood home is sold, a dutiful adult child's effort to help an aging parent sort boxes forces both to finally name the confession they have protected for years—changing what each is willing to keep, discard, or hand back.

## Short pitch

Eli returns home for one last night of packing. Mira wants the house cleaned out cleanly; Eli wants the job done without another family wound reopened. When the wrong box forces the old story into the room, each must decide whether to keep protecting the silence or finally speak and live with the cost.

## Central dramatic question

Before the house changes hands at dawn, will Eli and Mira name the avoided event and accept what it costs them, or will they bury it again and call the silence closure?

## External goal and failure consequences

- Protagonist: Eli.
- External goal: finish sorting the house before the closing and leave with a clear handoff of what to keep, what to discard, and what to return.
- Failure consequence: if Eli fails, the house closes with the secret still load-bearing, Eli shoulders the weight of that avoidance alone, and the parent-child bond narrows into another polite, careful arrangement.

## Internal need / wound / misbelief

- Need: to stop confusing protection with love and claim a voice in the family story.
- Wound: years ago, Mira confessed something that reshaped Eli’s understanding of the family, yet neither fully examined or repaired the damage.
- Misbelief: if the truth is handled quietly, the relationship will stay intact.

## Primary opposition

- Mira opposes Eli’s push toward disclosure with fatigue, minimization, deflection, and the practical urgency of clearing the house.
- The house and time reinforce the opposition: one night, diminishing light, heavy objects, final paperwork, and the pull of old roles.
- Eli’s own guilt and caretaking habits create inner opposition.

## Story engine (why this sustains target length)

- One night, one house, one central conflict.
- Sorting boxes forces objects, rooms, and memories into play without turning the story into a puzzle hunt.
- Each practical decision becomes an emotional pressure point.
- Dialogue works through subtext, task business, and withheld speech.
- Stakes escalate because the closing is irreversible and the last chance to speak is shrinking.

## Thematic question

What do we keep when keeping silence no longer protects the people we love?

## Distinctive story elements

- A box that functions as catalyst/pressure source, not MacGuffin.
- A confession or disclosure that reshaped the family yet was never fully addressed.
- A house used as container for escalating pressure, not decorative symbol.
- Two fully realized agents: the adult child complicit in avoidance, the parent still acting from her own reasons.
- An ending built on a hard choice with lasting consequence, not tidy reconciliation.

## Genre promise

- Contemporary literary realism.
- Domestic, compressed, emotionally honest.
- No gimmicks, no contrived reveals, no tidy moral lesson.
- Pressure earned through object, room, dialogue, and choice.

## Candidate notes (if comparative premises were reviewed)

- Selected candidate: Silence-as-structure domestic realism. The avoided event is a parental confession that reshaped the child’s understanding of the family, and the sorting night forces both characters to choose whether to keep protecting the silence or finally speak.
- Rejected alternatives summary:
  - Mystery-in-the-box variant: rejected because it risks making the box a puzzle rather than relational pressure.
  - Long-distance prodigal return variant: rejected because it weakens the one-night compression and caregiver friction.
  - Rival-sibling variant: rejected because it breaks the mandatory two-character design.
