# Original Prompt

> Preserve the user's prompt verbatim below. Do not edit after lock except via explicit change control.

```text
Write an original fantasy short story (target 4,000-5,000 words; about 4-6 scenes).

Premise:
A potion maker who creates potions for emotions is visited by someone who wants to forget grief. But the potion only works if you surrender the emotion you're trying to escape — and this person's grief is all they have left of someone they loved.

Constraints for this pilot:
- One central conflict only: whether (and how) they decide what to keep and what to let go.
- Cast: exactly two speaking characters (potion maker + customer). No new major characters.
- Locations: at most two (the potion shop + one other). Prefer mostly the shop.
- Timeline: roughly one evening, or a few hours.
- No subplot, romance arc, crime plot, supernatural twist, or institutional conspiracy.
- Tone: literary fantasy; contemplative but not bleak; PG-13; non-graphic.
- End on a hard character choice with lasting consequence (even if emotionally unresolved). Do not tidy it into a neat moral lesson.
- The magic system should feel grounded — clear rules, clear costs, no deus ex machina.

Story must show:
- concrete sensory grounding (smells of potions, textures of ingredients, light in the shop);
- dialogue with subtext (deflection, hesitation, things unsaid);
- clear goal / opposition / agency for the protagonist in each scene;
- causal scene-to-scene pressure so a reader wants the next scene.

Deliver a complete short story, not a novel outline.
```
