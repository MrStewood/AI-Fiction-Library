# Ending Design

## Climactic choice

Maren makes the potion. She has spent the entire story resisting the request — not because she cannot help, but because making it forces her to confront her own unspoken grief. In the final scene, she chooses to make it not because she believes it is right, but because she recognizes that Elias's choice is his own, and her refusal would be a form of control, not care. The act of making the potion is the internal change made visible: her hands move deliberately, not with the practiced ease of professional distance, but with the slow weight of someone who understands what she is doing.

## External resolution

Elias drinks the potion. The grief dissolves. His memories of his wife remain — her name, her face, the years they shared — but they are flat, factual, emptied of emotional weight. He does not mourn what he has lost because he no longer has the capacity to feel it. He leaves the shop. The transaction is complete.

## Internal transformation (shown through action)

Maren's transformation is shown through three actions:
1. **She makes the potion.** Not with professional pride, but with deliberate, heavy hands. She is no longer solving a problem — she is participating in an irreversible act.
2. **She does not drink her own potion.** After Elias leaves, she reaches for the bottle on the high shelf — the one she has never touched — and then stops. She keeps her grief. This is the cost: she let Elias lose his, but she keeps hers.
3. **She stands at the door.** The final image is Maren at the shop threshold, looking out at the street, holding the bottle she will not open. She has chosen to carry what she has always helped others set down.

## Subplot resolutions

None. The story has no subplots. The single thread — Maren's choice — is the only arc.

## Required setup

- SP-001: Maren's hands must be established as practiced and professional early, so the reader sees the shift when they hesitate.
- SP-002: The bottle on the high shelf must be visible but unexplained — a background detail that gains meaning at the end.
- SP-003: The smell of the shop must be grounded in the opening, so the final image (Maren standing in the doorway, the smell behind her) carries sensory weight.
- SP-004: Maren's professional questions must be established as routine, so the shift to personal questions is felt.
- SP-005: Elias's specific request must be clear — he is not forgetting grief in general, he is forgetting his wife.
- SP-006: The departing customer's line about "setting things down" plants the thematic question before the catalyst.
- SP-007: The ingredients on the counter must be visible and ordinary — the physical reality of making the potion.
- SP-008: Elias's line "I will remember everything. I just won't feel it." must be established in the conversation, so it echoes in the Finale.

## Cost / consequence

- **For Elias:** He loses the emotional weight of every memory of his wife. The facts remain; the love does not. He will not remember what it felt like to love her. This is irreversible.
- **For Maren:** She has participated in the destruction of something she knows has value. She has done this because she respects Elias's autonomy, not because she believes it is right. The consequence is that she must now live with what she has done — and with her own grief, which she has chosen not to surrender. The bottle she made for herself remains on the shelf. She will not drink it.
- **For the reader:** The story does not resolve the question of whether grief is the price of love or damage love leaves behind. The reader is left with the image of Maren holding the bottle she will not open — a person who has chosen to carry what she could set down.

## Final emotional effect

The reader should feel the weight of an irreversible choice — not tragedy, not triumph, but the quiet, heavy knowledge that both characters have been permanently changed. Maren's choice to keep her grief is not heroic; it is the only honest response to what she has done. The ending should feel like a door closing — not with a slam, but with a click.

## Final Image vs Opening Image

| Element | Opening Image | Final Image |
|---|---|---|
| **Maren's hands** | Practiced, professional, moving with ease through routine tasks | Holding the empty bottle Elias drank from — deliberate, heavy, still |
| **The shop** | A place of work — warm light, organized shelves, functional routine | A place of reckoning — the same light, the same shelves, but now charged with what has happened |
| **The smell** | Herbs, vinegar, metallic — the smell of her profession | The same smell — but now it is behind her, not around her; she has stepped out of it |
| **Maren's position** | Behind the counter, in control, the expert | At the door, on the threshold, no longer in control — she has chosen to stand between the shop and the street |
| **The bottle on the shelf** | Background detail, unexplained | The object she reaches for and does not take — the gesture that says everything about her choice |
