# Story Bible

## Characters

### Protagonist
- Name: Maren
- External goal: Maintain professional distance while deciding whether to make the grief-dissolution potion for Elias
- Internal need: Stop treating grief as a problem to be solved; accept it as evidence of love
- Wound / misbelief: She lost someone important years ago and never processed the grief. Instead, she built a career helping others manage theirs, keeping her own grief at a professional distance. Misbelief: "Grief is damage. Helping someone neutralize it is kindness."
- Arc: From professional detachment (grief is a problem she solves for others) to personal reckoning (grief is the evidence of love she has been avoiding in herself)

### Antagonist / opposition
No character antagonist. The opposition is the potion itself — a material object whose existence forces the choice. The antagonist is the irreversible transaction: the potion exists, it works, and using it cannot be undone. Maren's professional identity is aligned with making it; her personal history is aligned with refusing it.

### Supporting cast
| Name | Function | Arc notes |
|---|---|---|
| Elias | Customer / catalyst | Wants to forget his dead wife. His grief is raw and specific. He does not change — he is the mirror that forces Maren's change. |

## Relationship map

Maren ↔ Elias: Professional relationship across a counter. She is the expert; he is the supplicant. As the conversation progresses, the power dynamic shifts — he becomes the one who names what she has been avoiding. There is no romance, no friendship, no antagonism. The relationship is transactional until it becomes personal.

## World rules and limitations

### Magic system: Emotion potions
1. **Rule:** To forget an emotion, you must surrender it entirely. No partial versions exist.
2. **Cost:** You cannot choose which memories the emotion is attached to. The emotion goes, and the memories become flat, factual, emptied of weight.
3. **Scope:** The potion affects one specific emotion (grief, longing, fear, etc.), not all emotions.
4. **Irreversibility:** Once the emotion is surrendered, it cannot be recovered.
5. **Ingredients:** Grounded, non-precious — herbs, minerals, distillates. No rare or magical-sounding components.
6. **Craft:** The potion maker must prepare it by hand. There is no automation. The physical act of making it is part of the cost.

### Shop rules
- The shop is a real place with real inventory — not a metaphor.
- Potions have names, prices, and clear effects.
- Maren has the professional skill and authority to make or refuse any potion.
- She is not compelled by law, magic, or custom to fulfill every request.

## Locations

1. **Maren's potion shop** (primary): A small, narrow shop. Shelves of bottles. A counter. A back room where she works. The light is warm but not inviting — functional, professional. It smells of herbs, vinegar, and something faintly metallic.
2. **The street outside** (secondary, minimal): A brief exterior — evening, a door closing, the shop behind her. Used only for the final image.

## Chronology baseline

One evening. A few hours. Elias arrives near dusk; the conversation stretches into full dark. The story ends when he leaves.

## Cultural / historical constraints

- Not set in a specific historical period. The world feels timeless — no technology, no contemporary references.
- The potion shop exists in a place where such shops are understood, not remarkable.
- No institutional structures (government, church, guild) are referenced or needed.

## Ending facts that must be prepared

1. Maren makes the potion. (The story requires her to make it — the internal change is in how she makes it, not whether.)
2. Elias drinks it. (He does not hesitate. The grief is gone.)
3. The memories remain but are flat, factual, emptied of emotional weight.
4. Maren is left alone in the shop with the knowledge of what she has done.
5. Maren does not drink her own potion. (This is the cost — she chose to let Elias lose his grief, but she keeps hers.)
6. The final image is Maren standing at the shop door, looking at the street, holding a bottle — but not drinking.
