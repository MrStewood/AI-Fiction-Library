# The Potion Maker

Lamplight pooled on the worn wood. The ingredients waited in their neat row — charcoal, vinegar, bone ash, binding agent — the same row she'd abandoned an hour ago when she'd retreated to the back room and stood there doing nothing.

"I can make it." Maren's voice, flat, certain.

Elias said nothing. His palms lay open on the wood between them.

"It will take time."

Into the mortar she measured charcoal — dark dust the color of burnt bone settling against pale stone. The pestle turned in slow circles, grinding what was already ground, breaking down what did not need breaking. Stone on stone, a scrape as steady as breathing.

Uncorking the vial of vinegar with her teeth — a habit she'd never corrected — she let three drops fall into the bowl. Darkening. A sharp tang cut through the shop's permanent smell of dried herbs and old timber. Counterclockwise only, the pestle turning the way she'd learned by doing, years ago, from no one.

Bone ash next. The paper folded into a funnel, tapped gently — pale dust streamed down in a thin, steady column. Lightening now, the mixture taking on a milky quality, not quite finished, not quite raw.

Minutes passed. How many, she couldn't say. The mixture changed the way dawn changes dark to grey: subtly, then all at once, thinning, clarifying, the vinegar smell dissolving into something mineral and clean — water over stone.

The pestle set aside. Under the counter, a bottle waited — small, clear glass, unlabeled. She placed it on the wood beside the mortar, wiped the rim with a cloth, and tilted. A thin, steady stream of liquid — clear as water, catching the lamplight, ordinary in her hands — filled the bottle to the neck. A stopper pressed home.

Small enough to hold in one palm. Clear. Nothing about it announced what it could do.

Then: stillness. Her hands came to rest on the worn wood, palms down. Heavy. Not from the work — she hadn't been working long enough for that ache.

Above the register, where it had sat for years gathering dust she wiped away each morning without thinking, the dark bottle with its yellowed label caught the lamplight. She did not look up.

Across the wood, she slid the finished potion. A straight line, stopping at the edge of his hands. Glass on timber — a faint whisper, and then silence.

She did not pick the bottle back up. She had made it. Now it sat between them, occupying the exact space where her choice had lived.

The shop smelled of herbs, vinegar, and the mineral sharpness of what she had just done.

Elias looked at the bottle for a long time. He did not touch it.

Above the register, the dark bottle on the high shelf stared down. Maren's hands were still — palms down, heavy, the lamplight pooling on her fingers and on the small clear vessel that waited.

She had done this. It was finished. It could not be unmade.
