# Early Reader Checkpoint — SCENE-001

## Verdict

**continue**

## Desire-to-Continue Score

**5 / 5**

I want to keep reading. The scene ends with Maren alone in the dark, the ingredients waiting, the choice suspended. The deferral to tomorrow creates anticipation. The bottle on the shelf is the most compelling planted detail — I want to know what it is, why she made it, and why she never used it.

## What Hooked

- **The shop.** The sensory grounding is immediate and precise. Rosemary, vinegar, copper, charcoal. The lamp casting its warm circle. The bottles on the shelves. I feel the space. I trust the writer.
- **Maren's competence.** Her hands move without searching. She lays out ingredients precisely. She tells Elias to come back tomorrow — not no, not yet. She controls the pace. I trust her.
- **Elias's singing memory.** "Stupid songs. Made-up words. Melodies that didn't go anywhere." This is the scene's best line. It makes me feel what he's losing without the writer telling me to feel it.
- **The bottle on the shelf.** The scene's final image. Maren made it for herself, years ago, and never used it. She doesn't touch it. This is the hook. I need to know.
- **The closing line.** "She didn't touch the bottle." Simple, final, resonant. The scene ends where it should.

## What Stalled

Nothing stalled. The scene is controlled, close, and assured. The pacing is deliberate but never sluggish. For the target reader — literary fantasy, contemplative, character-driven — this is exactly what the brief promises.

## Story Promise Visibility

The story promise is clearly visible. The brief promises:

- **A complete moral decision with lasting consequence.** The grief potion exists. Its rules are named. Elias wants it. Maren can refuse. The choice is coming.
- **Clear rules and costs.** "Memory stays. Emotion goes. You can't undo it." The magic system is grounded and concrete.
- **An ending that is hard and honest.** The bottle on the shelf signals that Maren has her own history of loss. The story will not be tidy.

The reader trusts that the writer knows where this is going.

## Confusion Points

**Minor: the two bells.** The scene mentions "the small bell on the shelf behind the register" and "not the broken one above the door." The two-bell setup is clear enough, but the broken bell is not introduced before it's referenced. One sentence of grounding earlier (e.g., Maren noticing the broken bell when she enters the shop) would resolve this. Not a confusion point for the target reader — more a continuity note.

**No other confusion points.** The world is clear. The rules are clear. The characters are clear. The conflict is specific.

## Expected vs. Delivered Promises

All promises for this gate are met. The scene establishes:

- The world (the shop, the potions, the magic system)
- The characters (Maren's competence, Elias's grief)
- The conflict (the specific request, the irreversible cost)
- The thematic question (is pain the price of love or its proof?)

The scene defers the choice to tomorrow. This is appropriate — it's a professional assessment protocol — but the reader should expect the next scene to continue the conversation and deepen the moral confrontation.

## Recommendation

**Continue to SCENE-002.** The opening is strong. The story promise is visible. The prose is controlled. No revision required at this gate.
