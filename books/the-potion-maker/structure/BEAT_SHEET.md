# Save the Cat Beat Sheet

Length profile: short_story
Beat-sheet mode: compressed (short_story/novelette — landmarks tightly merged, causality explicit)

For each beat: functional purpose; planned event/sequence; responsible character; causal inputs; consequence; internal-arc movement; setups/payoffs; approx manuscript position; related scene IDs.

## 1. Opening Image

- **Functional purpose:** Establish the world, the protagonist's routine, and the emotional baseline before the catalyst arrives. Show Maren in control, professional, skilled — the person who manages other people's grief for a living.
- **Planned event/sequence:** Maren is working in the shop at dusk. She is grinding an ingredient — something rough and dry — with a mortar and pestle. The shop smells of herbs, vinegar, and a faint metallic tang. Shelves of bottles line the walls. She is efficient, practiced, alone. A customer (minor, non-speaking) leaves; Maren counts payment. The shop is her territory.
- **Responsible character:** Maren
- **Causal inputs:** None (opening)
- **Consequence:** The reader understands who Maren is, what the shop is, and the emotional register of the world.
- **Internal-arc movement:** Maren is in the "before" state: professional distance, grief as a problem she solves for others.
- **Setups/payoffs:** SP-001 (hands move with practiced ease), SP-003 (shop smell grounded), SP-002 (bottle on high shelf visible in background — not mentioned, just there)
- **Approx manuscript position:** 0–10%
- **Related scene IDs:** SCENE-001

## 2. Theme Stated

- **Functional purpose:** Surface the thematic question without naming it. A line of dialogue or a moment that plants the seed.
- **Planned event/sequence:** The departing customer (minor) says something offhand — something about grief, about forgetting, about how people come to Maren to "set things down." Maren's response is professional, deflecting. But the word "settle" or "set down" echoes. The theme is: Is grief the price of love, or damage that love leaves behind?
- **Responsible character:** Maren (and the departing customer)
- **Causal inputs:** The customer's comment is the first hint that grief is not just a product Maren sells — it is a currency she trades in.
- **Consequence:** The reader hears the thematic question before the story asks it directly.
- **Internal-arc movement:** None yet — Maren is still in routine mode.
- **Setups/payoffs:** SP-004 (Maren's professional deflection established)
- **Approx manuscript position:** 10–15%
- **Related scene IDs:** SCENE-001

## 3. Setup

- **Functional purpose:** Establish the world's rules through action, not exposition. Show how the shop works, what potions do, what the cost is. Lock the reader into the physical reality of the place.
- **Planned event/sequence:** Maren tidies the counter, restocks a bottle. The reader sees the mechanics: potions have labels, prices, effects. The shop is not metaphorical — it is a business. Maren's hands move with ease. She pauses near a particular bottle on a high shelf (SP-002) — a half-second, nothing more — then continues.
- **Responsible character:** Maren
- **Causal inputs:** The routine establishes the norm that will be disrupted.
- **Consequence:** The reader understands the magic system's grounding (emotion as commodity) and Maren's professional rhythm.
- **Internal-arc movement:** Maren is comfortable, in control, in her element.
- **Setups/payoffs:** SP-001 (hands), SP-002 (bottle on shelf), SP-003 (shop smell)
- **Approx manuscript position:** 15–25%
- **Related scene IDs:** SCENE-001

## 4. Catalyst

- **Functional purpose:** The event that disrupts the protagonist's equilibrium and introduces the central dramatic question.
- **Planned event/sequence:** The bell above the door. Elias enters. He is not a regular. He stands at the counter and asks, in a flat voice, for the grief-dissolution potion. He specifies: not grief in general — grief for his wife. She died. He wants to forget her. Not the facts — the feeling.
- **Responsible character:** Elias
- **Causal inputs:** Elias's request is the catalyst. It is specific (his wife, not grief abstractly) and personal (he has tried everything else).
- **Consequence:** Maren's professional routine is broken. The story's engine starts: will she make the potion?
- **Internal-arc movement:** Maren shifts from routine to assessment. She begins the professional protocol — questions, evaluation — but the request lands differently than others.
- **Setups/payoffs:** SP-005 (Elias's specific request)
- **Approx manuscript position:** 25–30%
- **Related scene IDs:** SCENE-001 / SCENE-002

## 5. Debate

- **Functional purpose:** The protagonist resists the call to action. The internal conflict surfaces — not through declaration, but through behavior.
- **Planned event/sequence:** Maren asks her standard questions: When did she die? How long has the grief lasted? Has he tried other potions (numbing, sleep aids, etc.)? Elias answers, but his answers are specific — he names his wife, he describes what the grief feels like. Maren's questions become less standard. She pauses. She asks something she does not usually ask: "What do you remember about her?" The question is professional — she needs to know the scope of the emotion — but it is also personal. Elias's answer is quiet, detailed, concrete. Maren's hands stop moving.
- **Responsible character:** Maren
- **Causal inputs:** Elias's specific answers force Maren to confront the reality of what the potion does — it does not erase grief in general; it erases the love that produced the grief.
- **Consequence:** Maren's professional distance begins to crack. The reader sees that this request is different for her — not because of the customer, but because of what she carries.
- **Internal-arc movement:** Maren moves from routine assessment to reluctant engagement. She is no longer just processing a transaction; she is being confronted with her own avoidance.
- **Setups/payoffs:** SP-004 (professional questions shift to personal)
- **Approx manuscript position:** 30–45%
- **Related scene IDs:** SCENE-002

## 6. Break into Two

- **Functional purpose:** The protagonist commits to Act Two — she engages with the request instead of deflecting it. The decision is not to make the potion yet, but to stop treating the request as routine.
- **Planned event/sequence:** Maren tells Elias she will need to prepare the ingredients. She does not refuse; she does not promise. She steps into the back room. The reader sees her there — alone, hands on the counter, breathing. She does not reach for the ingredients. She stands still. Then she begins.
- **Responsible character:** Maren
- **Causal inputs:** The debate has pushed Maren past the point of professional detachment. She cannot treat this as routine.
- **Consequence:** The story shifts from "will she consider it?" to "what will she do?"
- **Internal-arc movement:** Maren commits to engaging with the request, not because she has resolved her conflict, but because she cannot avoid it.
- **Setups/payoffs:** None
- **Approx manuscript position:** 45–50%
- **Related scene IDs:** SCENE-002

## 7. B Story

- **Functional purpose:** In a standard screenplay, the B Story carries the theme through a secondary relationship. In this compressed short story, there is no B Story. The thematic material is carried by the A Story — the conversation between Maren and Elias.
- **Planned event/sequence:** N/A — no B Story. The theme is embedded in the main conversation.
- **Responsible character:** N/A
- **Causal inputs:** N/A
- **Consequence:** N/A
- **Internal-arc movement:** N/A
- **Setups/payoffs:** N/A
- **Approx manuscript position:** N/A
- **Related scene IDs:** N/A

## 8. Fun and Games

- **Functional purpose:** The "promise of the premise" — the scenes where the reader gets what they came for. Here, the "fun" is the emotional escalation: the conversation deepens, the cost becomes concrete, and Maren's internal conflict becomes visible.
- **Planned event/sequence:** Maren returns from the back room. She begins to prepare the potion — not to make it yet, but to show Elias what it involves. She lays out the ingredients: ground charcoal, vinegar, a pale powder. She explains the cost: the potion will take the grief, but it will also take the emotional weight of every memory connected to it. She asks Elias if he understands. He says he does. Maren asks: "Will you remember her name?" Elias says: "I will remember everything. I just won't feel it."
- **Responsible character:** Maren and Elias
- **Causal inputs:** The cost is now named explicitly. The reader understands the full scope of what Elias is asking for.
- **Consequence:** The stakes are concrete. The reader can feel the weight of the choice.
- **Internal-arc movement:** Maren is now fully engaged. She is no longer assessing — she is participating.
- **Setups/payoffs:** SP-001 (hands move with practiced ease — but now the practice is deliberate, not routine)
- **Approx manuscript position:** 50–65%
- **Related scene IDs:** SCENE-003

## 9. Midpoint

- **Functional purpose:** The midpoint must materially change knowledge, position, strategy, stakes, or relationship to the conflict. Here, Maren's internal conflict becomes external — she reveals (indirectly) that she understands the cost of the potion not because she is a professional, but because she has considered using it herself.
- **Planned event/sequence:** Maren pauses. Her hands are on the counter. She asks Elias a question that is not part of the standard protocol: "Have you ever made one of these for yourself?" The question is directed at Elias, but it is really about her. Elias looks at her. He says: "No. That's your job." The answer is not hostile — it is honest. He has come to her because she is the one who knows how to do this. Maren's hands stop. The reader sees — through action, not interiority — that Maren has a bottle on a high shelf (SP-002) that she has never opened. The midpoint changes everything: Maren is no longer just a professional deciding whether to fulfill a request. She is a person confronting the fact that she has the same request inside her.
- **Responsible character:** Maren
- **Causal inputs:** Maren's question to Elias is the moment she breaks her own protocol. It is the first time she has asked a customer something that reveals her own history.
- **Consequence:** The power dynamic shifts. Elias is no longer just a customer — he is a mirror. Maren's professional identity is no longer sufficient to carry the weight of the choice.
- **Internal-arc movement:** Maren moves from reluctant engagement to personal reckoning. She can no longer pretend this is just a transaction.
- **Setups/payoffs:** SP-002 (bottle on shelf — now charged with meaning), SP-004 (professional questions become personal)
- **Approx manuscript position:** 65–70%
- **Related scene IDs:** SCENE-003

## 10. Bad Guys Close In

- **Functional purpose:** The opposition tightens. The protagonist's internal resistance and external pressure converge. Here, the "bad guy" is the potion itself — its existence, its irreversibility, and the fact that Maren has the power to make it.
- **Planned event/sequence:** Maren tells Elias she can make the potion. She says it will take time — she needs to prepare. She begins. The reader watches her hands move: grinding, mixing, measuring. The ingredients are ordinary. The act is physical, deliberate. Elias watches. Maren's hands do not hesitate — but the reader knows, from the midpoint, that she is making something she has considered making for herself. The act of making it for Elias is also an act of confronting her own avoidance.
- **Responsible character:** Maren
- **Causal inputs:** Maren has committed to making the potion. The midpoint has revealed her personal stake, but she continues. The pressure is now internal — she is doing something she knows has cost, for someone else, while carrying the same cost herself.
- **Consequence:** The potion is being made. The choice is narrowing. The reader feels the irreversibility approaching.
- **Internal-arc movement:** Maren is acting against her own avoidance. She is making the potion for Elias, but she is also making it for herself — not to drink, but to confront.
- **Setups/payoffs:** SP-001 (hands move — but now the practice is heavy, not light)
- **Approx manuscript position:** 70–80%
- **Related scene IDs:** SCENE-004

## 11. All Is Lost

- **Functional purpose:** The moment where it seems the protagonist has failed — not externally, but internally. Here, All Is Lost arises from Maren's prior choices and pressures: she has made the potion. She has done the thing she has been avoiding. There is no undoing it.
- **Planned event/sequence:** The potion is finished. It sits on the counter between them. Maren looks at it. The reader sees her hands — still, heavy, resting on the counter. She has done this. She has made the potion that she has considered making for herself. She has done it for someone else. The cost is now real, not abstract. There is a moment of silence. Maren does not pick up the bottle. She slides it across the counter to Elias.
- **Responsible character:** Maren
- **Causal inputs:** The potion exists because Maren chose to make it. The All Is Lost moment is that she cannot unmake it. The choice is irreversible — not because of magic, but because of what she has done.
- **Consequence:** The potion is on the counter. Elias can take it or leave it. Maren has done her part. The cost is now shared.
- **Internal-arc movement:** Maren has hit the bottom of her internal arc — she has confronted her avoidance by participating in the very thing she was avoiding. The grief she carries is now joined by the knowledge that she has helped someone else surrender theirs.
- **Setups/payoffs:** SP-001 (hands — still, heavy), SP-002 (bottle on shelf — she has made another one, but she will not make one for herself)
- **Approx manuscript position:** 80–85%
- **Related scene IDs:** SCENE-004

## 12. Dark Night of the Soul

- **Functional purpose:** The protagonist processes the All Is Lost moment. Here, the processing is silent — it happens in the pause between Maren sliding the bottle across the counter and Elias taking it.
- **Planned event/sequence:** Elias looks at the bottle. He does not touch it immediately. He looks at Maren. He says something simple — not a question, not a statement, but something that acknowledges what has happened. Maybe: "Thank you." Or maybe nothing at all. Maren does not respond. The reader sees her hands — still on the counter, not reaching for anything. The shop smells the same. The light is the same. Nothing has changed except everything.
- **Responsible character:** Maren
- **Causal inputs:** The All Is Lost moment has landed. Maren is now in the space between having done the thing and facing what it means.
- **Consequence:** The reader feels the weight of the choice — not through dialogue, but through silence and stillness.
- **Internal-arc movement:** Maren is in the darkest moment: she has done the thing she was avoiding, and there is no taking it back.
- **Setups/payoffs:** SP-003 (shop smell — now the smell is the smell of what she has done, not what she does)
- **Approx manuscript position:** 85–88%
- **Related scene IDs:** SCENE-004

## 13. Break into Three

- **Functional purpose:** The protagonist synthesizes Act One (professional distance) and Act Two (personal reckoning) into a new understanding. Here, Maren's new understanding is shown through action: she does not stop Elias from taking the potion. She does not drink her own. She lets the choice be what it is.
- **Planned event/sequence:** Elias takes the bottle. He drinks it. The reader watches — Maren watches — as the grief leaves him. His face does not change. He sets the bottle down. He looks at the shop — the shelves, the bottles, the counter. He looks at Maren. He says something ordinary — something about the weather, or the time, or the street outside. The grief is gone. The memories are flat. He turns to leave.
- **Responsible character:** Maren and Elias
- **Causal inputs:** The potion has worked. The grief is gone. The cost is real. Maren has watched it happen.
- **Consequence:** Elias is changed. Maren has witnessed it. The story's central question has been answered — not with a lesson, but with a fact.
- **Internal-arc movement:** Maren moves from the dark night into acceptance: this is what the potion does. This is what she has helped create. She does not regret it — but she does not celebrate it.
- **Setups/payoffs:** SP-005 (Elias's specific request — fulfilled)
- **Approx manuscript position:** 88–93%
- **Related scene IDs:** SCENE-005

## 14. Finale

- **Functional purpose:** The protagonist demonstrates internal change through action. Here, Maren's action is what she does after Elias leaves.
- **Planned event/sequence:** Elias leaves. The bell above the door rings. The shop is quiet. Maren stands behind the counter. She looks at the bottle Elias drank from — empty, on the counter. She looks at the shelves. She looks at the bottle on the high shelf (SP-002) — the one she has never touched. She reaches for it. Her hand closes around it. She holds it. She does not open it. She sets it back. She walks to the door. She stands in the doorway. She looks at the street. She is holding something — maybe the empty bottle, maybe nothing. She does not drink. She does not cry. She stands there.
- **Responsible character:** Maren
- **Causal inputs:** The potion has been made and used. Maren has witnessed the cost. Now she faces her own grief — the one she has carried for years, the one she has never processed.
- **Consequence:** Maren's internal change is shown through three actions: (1) she made the potion for Elias, (2) she did not make one for herself, (3) she stands at the door, carrying what she has always helped others set down.
- **Internal-arc movement:** Maren has completed her arc: from professional detachment to personal reckoning to acceptance. She does not resolve her grief — she chooses to carry it.
- **Setups/payoffs:** SP-001 (hands — holding, not working), SP-002 (bottle on shelf — reached for, not taken), SP-003 (shop smell — the smell of what she has done, behind her now), SP-004 (questions — no more questions, just silence)
- **Approx manuscript position:** 93–98%
- **Related scene IDs:** SCENE-005

## 15. Final Image

- **Functional purpose:** The final image must meaningfully contrast the Opening Image. It shows the protagonist transformed — not through declaration, but through position, action, and sensory detail.
- **Planned event/sequence:** Maren stands in the doorway of the shop. The street is dark. The shop is behind her — warm light, shelves of bottles, the counter where she made the potion. She is on the threshold. She is not inside (where she was in control) and not outside (where she would be free of it). She is between. The smell of the shop is behind her — herbs, vinegar, metallic. She is holding the empty bottle from Elias's drink. She does not raise it. She does not drop it. She holds it. The final image is: Maren, at the door, holding what cannot be undone, carrying what she will not set down.
- **Responsible character:** Maren
- **Causal inputs:** The entire story has led to this image. The Opening Image showed Maren in control, behind the counter, in the shop. The Final Image shows her at the threshold, no longer in control, carrying the cost of what she has done.
- **Consequence:** The reader sees the transformation. Maren has moved from professional distance to personal reckoning to acceptance. She does not resolve the thematic question — she embodies it.
- **Internal-arc movement:** Complete. Maren has chosen to carry her grief rather than surrender it. This is not heroic — it is the only honest response to what she has done.
- **Setups/payoffs:** All five setups pay off in this image. SP-001 (hands — holding the empty bottle), SP-002 (bottle on shelf — not taken), SP-003 (shop smell — behind her now), SP-004 (questions — replaced by silence), SP-005 (Elias's request — fulfilled, the cost is real)
- **Approx manuscript position:** 98–100%
- **Related scene IDs:** SCENE-005
